# Briefing method

## Finding developments

Search by category × industry × geography × last 7 days. Useful source types:
- **Primary**: company newsrooms and investor relations, regulators (e.g. RBI, SEBI, MeitY in India; CBUAE, DFSA in the UAE; SEC, FTC in the US; EU Commission), stock-exchange filings.
- **Business press**: Economic Times, Mint, Business Standard, Moneycontrol (India); Gulf News, The National, Arabian Business (GCC); Reuters, Bloomberg, FT, WSJ, TechCrunch (global).
- **Trackers and newsletters**: Inc42, Entrackr, YourStory (India startups); MAGNiTT, Wamda (MENA); Crunchbase News; sector newsletters. Use them to discover, then confirm with a primary or press source.

## Ranking by business significance (not media attention)

Score each development 1–5 on: **scale** (money, users, market share affected), **breadth** (how many companies/customers it touches), **permanence** (one-off vs structural), **proximity** (how directly it touches the user's target role and geography). Rank by the total; break ties by recency.

Typical high-significance items: large M&A, regulation that changes how the industry operates, platform shifts (e.g. AI changing a core workflow), major player entering or exiting a market, big funding rounds that create a new well-capitalised competitor, sustained layoffs or hiring sprees at bellwethers.

## Interpretation frame

For each top development:
- **Immediate**: who must act now, what changes in budgets, roadmaps or customer behaviour this quarter.
- **6–12 months**: competitive responses, consolidation, pricing, hiring and skills shifts.
- **Long-term**: market structure, business models, which capabilities become table stakes.
- **Leadership perspective**: what's on the CEO/board agenda (growth, margin, risk, regulation, talent), the worry (disruption, cost, compliance), the opportunity (new segment, efficiency, partnership), and the likely decisions (build/buy/partner, enter/exit, invest/cut).

## Hiring lens

Map developments to talent demand:
- Funding/expansion → engineering, product, sales, and local leadership roles expand.
- Regulation → compliance, risk, security, data-governance roles expand.
- Consolidation/M&A → integration roles expand; duplicate functions shrink.
- Automation/AI adoption → roles that build, integrate and govern AI expand; routine execution roles shrink.
- Margin pressure → efficiency, platform and cost-optimisation roles expand; experimental teams shrink.
State which functions, skills, leadership capabilities and roles rise or decline, and why.

## Heat signals

- **Rising**: repeated across several developments this week and recent weeks.
- **Emerging**: early, few players, but backed by money, regulation or a credible leader.
- **Potential disruptions**: low probability, high impact; name the trigger to watch.
- **Risks**: things that could hurt the user's target market or role (freezes, regulation, consolidation).
Only include signals with at least one dated development behind them.

## Competitor intelligence

- **Gaining momentum**: funding, launches, customer wins, market share gains.
- **Under pressure**: layoffs, losses, regulatory action, leadership exits, down rounds.
- **Investing aggressively**: capex, acquisitions, hiring sprees, new geographies.
- **Likely to create hiring opportunities**: overlap of momentum/investment with the user's function and geography.

## Talking points

Each is one or two sentences: an observation plus a "so what", tied to a development, phrased as the user would say it in conversation. Mix: market structure, customer behaviour, technology, regulation, talent. Avoid hedged generalities and hype words.

## What this means for the user

Tie to the target role and seniority:
- **Opportunities**: companies or teams likely to hire for the role because of this week's developments.
- **Skills**: specific capabilities rising in demand that the user's resume shows gaps in (check the resume).
- **Themes to understand**: 2–3 topics worth an hour of reading this week.
- **Companies to monitor**: with the trigger to watch.
- **Risks**: developments that could reduce demand for the role or shift it to other locations.
