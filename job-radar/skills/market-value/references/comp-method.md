# Compensation benchmarking method

## 0. Normalise the role first

- **Level**: map the title + years + scope to the company's likely level (e.g. Senior / Staff / Lead / Manager). Titles vary by company; scope wins.
- **Basis**: annual, gross, in local currency. Decide and state whether figures include variable pay and equity (e.g. Indian "CTC" often includes variable, retirement contributions and sometimes ESOP value; Gulf packages are usually monthly and tax-free with allowances; US uses base + target bonus + RSU/yr).
- **Equity**: value listed RSUs at current price over the vesting period; value private-company ESOPs conservatively (often treat as 0–25% of grant value) and say so.

## 1. Target company

Inputs: company size, listing/funding stage, profitability, market position, geography (HQ vs local hub, capability centre vs product HQ), function's importance to the business, recent hiring push or layoffs, known pay philosophy.

For each component (fixed, variable/bonus, long-term incentives, total):
- **Conservative**: lower band for the level, low bonus payout, minimal equity value.
- **Most likely**: median of the credible signals for this level at this company (or its closest proxy).
- **Aggressive**: top of band, strong bonus, full equity value; achievable with competing offers or rare skills (do not state how to negotiate).
Explain each with the signals behind it.

Proxies when company data is thin: same company in another city adjusted by local market ratio; peers of the same size/stage in the same geography; the company's posted bands elsewhere.

## 2. Competitors

Pick 4–6: direct product competitors, companies competing for the same talent in the same city, and companies of similar stage/size. For each: fixed, variable, total (most likely) and a tier:
- **Highest paying**: global big tech, top-tier funded companies, HFT/quant, some US-headquartered capability centres.
- **Above market**: well-funded scale-ups, profitable product companies, premium MNCs.
- **Market**: established product companies, most MNC centres, mid-stage startups.
- **Below market**: services/consulting firms, early or cash-constrained startups, non-tech companies with in-house teams.
Say why premium payers pay more (equity, profitability, talent competition, HQ benchmarks).

## 3. Market benchmark (same geography)

Tiers: Tier 1 (top product/big tech), Tier 2 (mid-size product, established startups), fast-growing (recently funded), multinationals/capability centres, domestic firms (e.g. Indian services and enterprises where relevant).

Percentiles per tier: **P25 · median · P75 · top quartile (≈P90)**. Build from the signals; if only a median is available, use typical spreads (P25 ≈ 0.8× median, P75 ≈ 1.2–1.3×, top quartile ≈ 1.4–1.6×) and say you did.

What separates the levels: scope (IC vs leading a team, systems owned), scarcity of skills, brand/pedigree of past employers, demonstrated business impact, competing offers in hand, interview performance at the level.

## 4. Synthesis

- **Target company range**: conservative → aggressive (total).
- **Competitor range**: low → high of competitor most-likely totals.
- **Market range**: P25 → P75 of the relevant tiers.
- **Most likely**: overlap of the target-company likely band and the market median band, adjusted for the user's level and evidence.
- **Stretch**: top of target company aggressive ∩ market top quartile; state what profile reaches it.
Show the derivation in 2–4 bullets.

## 5. Positioning insight

- Company pays **below / at / above** market for this level (with evidence).
- What drives pay here: **brand value** (pays less, career signal), **bonus potential**, **promotion velocity**, **long-term incentives** (RSUs/ESOPs).
- How the role compares with similar opportunities: total and mix (cash vs equity vs bonus), stability of the pay components.

## Source quality ladder (strongest first)

1. Posted bands from the company itself (dated).
2. Recent offer data at the same level with sample size (crowd-sourced, ≤12 months).
3. Recruitment-firm salary guides for the geography/function (current year).
4. Older or small-sample crowd data; generic salary sites (signals only, never the sole basis).
5. Inference from company stage and peers (label as assumption).

## Geography notes

- **India**: CTC in ₹ lakh per annum; variable typically 10–20% for senior ICs at product companies; ESOPs common at startups; capability centres of US firms often pay at or above Tier 2 product companies; NCR, Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune differ modestly.
- **UAE / GCC**: monthly packages in AED/SAR, tax-free; basic + housing + transport (+ schooling/flights at senior levels); annual bonus 0–3 months' basic; equity rarer outside funded startups.
- **US**: base + target bonus % + annual RSU value; strong variation by city tier and level.
- **Remote roles**: pay often anchored to the employer's location bands with geographic adjustment; state which anchor is assumed.
