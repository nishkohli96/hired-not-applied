# Outreach principles (shared by all networking skills)

Voice and wording rules live in `voice.md`; read both.

## The five rules

1. **One person, one hook.** Every message references one specific, verifiable thing about the recipient or their team (a post, launch, talk, article, shared background). No hook → research first (`find-contacts` / web search) or ask the user.
2. **One ask.** Small and easy to say yes to: connect, a 15-minute chat, a pointer to the right person, a referral. Never stack asks.
3. **Lead with them, not you.** First line is about the recipient. The user's credentials come second, compressed to one line with one number.
4. **Short.** Channel limits below are ceilings, not targets.
5. **Honest.** Never invent shared connections, claims of having met, mutual interests, or the user's achievements.

## Channel limits (verify; platforms change them)

| Channel | Limit | Target |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn connection note | 300 chars (Premium); free accounts are capped lower (~200) and get few personalized notes per month | ≤200 chars |
| LinkedIn InMail | Subject 200 chars, body 1,900 chars | 60–120 words |
| LinkedIn DM (after connecting) | ~8,000 chars | 40–100 words |
| Cold email | none | 80–120 words direct, 120–200 story-led; subject 3–7 words |
| Follow-up | — | 25–60 words |

## Personalization hierarchy (strongest first)

1. Shared real connection (same former employer, school, community, referral from a named person who agreed).
2. Their recent work: post, talk, article, product launch, open-source repo, publication, case.
3. Team/company news tied to the role.
4. Shared field or problem space.
5. Generic admiration (avoid; last resort).

## Recipient types and what they want

| Recipient | They care about | Best ask |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiter / TA | Fit to open reqs, quick qualifying info | "Am I a fit for req X? Here are the 3 facts you need." |
| Hiring manager | Someone who solves their problem | Short chat about the team's challenge |
| Peer / internal employee | Not being used; low effort | Direct referral ask with a paste-ready summary, or advice first |
| Alumni / ex-colleague | Shared identity | Catch-up + advice; referral if warm |
| Senior leader / founder | Time, signal | One crisp idea or result relevant to them; pointer to right person |
| Not hiring / informational | Learning exchange | 15-min advice chat |

## Tone by domain and region

- Tech/startups: casual-professional, first names, no "Dear Sir/Madam".
- Finance, law, healthcare, government, academia: more formal; titles (Dr., Prof.) where used; complete sentences.
- Creative/design/media: lead with portfolio link and a specific work they made.
- Sales: brevity and value; they will judge the message as a sales email.
- Gulf, Japan, Korea, Germany: more formal greetings, surnames until invited otherwise.
- India/US/UK/AU tech: first names are fine.
- Write in the recipient's working language when the user can follow up in it.

## Timing

- Send Tue–Thu, 8–10 am in the recipient's time zone; avoid Fridays, weekends, local holidays.
- Follow-ups: day 3–4, day 7–10, final at day 14–21. Stop after 3 touches with no reply (across all channels combined).
- **Busy periods**: hold non-urgent outreach during the recipient's crunch: quarter/year-end close, annual appraisal cycles, major launches they've announced, and regional holiday seasons (e.g. Diwali week in India, Ramadan and Eid in the Gulf, late Dec–early Jan in the US/UK/EU). Hiring budgets often open in Jan–Feb and after funding news; that's a good window.
- **Trigger events**: time the first message to a fresh reason to write: their new role or promotion, a funding round, a launch, a new office, a post they wrote, a role just opened on their team, or a result the user just achieved. Reference the trigger in the first line.

## Mixing channels

Use more than one channel, but never the same message twice and never more than one touch on the same day:

| Day | Channel | Touch |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | LinkedIn | Connection note |
| 2 | Work email (if verified) | Cold email with the full hook and proof |
| 4–5 | LinkedIn | DM if they accepted (shorter, different angle); InMail if not and the user has credits |
| 8–10 | Same channel as their most likely reply (email thread or LinkedIn) | Value-add follow-up |
| 15–21 | One channel only | Graceful close |

- Phone or WhatsApp only when the person has shared their number for work (e.g. a recruiter who messaged first, or a number on their professional profile meant for contact), or after they've replied. Never cold-call a hiring manager's personal number.
- If they reply on any channel, stop the sequence on all others and continue there.

## Easy calls to action

- Make the yes take seconds: offer 2–3 specific time windows with time zone, or a scheduling link (Calendly, Cal.com, Google appointment page) if the user has one.
- Give them something to look at instead of a long message: a portfolio, GitHub, one-page profile or short case study link (the job seeker's "landing page").
- One CTA per message.

## Social proof

Third-party words beat self-description. When the user has them, use one short line from a LinkedIn recommendation, award, client quote or performance review (with the source named) in longer emails or after a positive reply. Never paraphrase a quote into something stronger than it was.

## Never

- Attach a resume to a first cold message to a hiring manager or employee (link instead). Recruiters/HR are the exception.
- Use any phrase or structure on the banned list in `voice.md`, "Dear Hiring Manager", or fake "Re:"/"Fwd:" subject lines.
- Ask a stranger for a referral without a forwardable summary and clear evidence of fit; a cold referral ask must make saying yes take two minutes.
- Guess and send to unverified email addresses in bulk.
- Send automatically. Drafts only; the user sends (unless they explicitly ask to send through a connected email tool).
