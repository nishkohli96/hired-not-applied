# Reading a hiring manager

## 1. Business context: what they're accountable for

| Signal | Likely accountability / pressure |
|---|---|
| Title scope (team, product, region, P&L) | The metric they're judged on: delivery, uptime, revenue, cost, growth, quality, compliance |
| Joined or promoted ≤12 months ago | Early wins, reshaping the team, proving the hire decisions |
| Long tenure (4+ years) | Stability, institutional trust, carrying legacy systems or processes |
| Company raised / launched / expanded recently | Scaling delivery and team fast; hiring pressure |
| Company had layoffs, cost focus, or flat results | Doing more with less; reliability and efficiency over experiments |
| New leader above them | Re-planning, re-justifying headcount and priorities |
| Their posts/talks | What they want to be known for; often the initiative they're driving |
| Team or site being built (e.g. new capability centre) | Hiring volume, onboarding, culture setting, process from scratch |

Pressure types: **operational** (delivery, incidents, process), **commercial** (revenue, cost, customers), **technical** (architecture, debt, scale, security), **leadership** (hiring, retention, team performance, stakeholder alignment).

## 2. Hiring mindset

- **What they value**: infer from their own path (what got them promoted), their posts, and the company stage. Builders who scaled teams value ownership and pace; leaders in regulated or mature businesses value reliability and judgement.
- **Dislikes in interviews**: vague answers without numbers, blaming past teams, overselling breadth without depth, not knowing the product, asking about perks before the work, monologues.
- **Credibility signals for them**: specific numbers, named systems or clients at comparable scale, trade-offs explained, admitting what didn't work, questions that show the candidate understood their business.
- **Risks they'll test for**: can this person do the job at our scale, ramp quickly, work with our stakeholders, stay, and raise the team's bar? For leads: can they lead without being the bottleneck? For candidates from services firms moving to product (or vice versa): ownership vs delivery mindset.
- **Expectations of the role**: what a strong hire delivers in 90 days and 12 months, inferred from their accountability.

## 3. Strategic opportunity

Map the user's proof bank to the manager's likely problems:
- Problem → user's closest evidence → how to phrase it as relevant (outcome + scale + what they'd do here).
- Choose 2–3 **themes** (from value statements if present) that line up with the manager's pressures.
- **Value-add angles**: a specific observation, pattern or lesson the user can offer from similar work, discussed as a peer, not pitched.

## 4. Conversation prep

- **Exploratory questions**: about their priorities, trade-offs and definition of success. Shapes: "What does the team need to be true in 12 months that isn't today?", "Where does <initiative> get hard that people outside don't see?", "What separates people who do well on your team in the first six months?"
- **Observations**: informed, specific, slightly opinionated, tied to their company's situation.
- **Avoid / careful**: compensation and perks early, criticism of their product or past employers, their company's layoffs or bad press unless they raise it, overclaiming leadership titles the user hasn't held.
- **Common mistakes with this type**:
  - Builder/scaling manager: being too process-heavy or slow to get to outcomes.
  - Operations/reliability manager: pitching novelty over stability, hand-waving incidents.
  - Commercial/business-facing manager: talking tech detail without the business result.
  - Technical-depth manager: staying high-level, unable to go two levels deep.
  - New-to-role manager: not asking about their plan; positioning as a lone hero instead of someone who helps them hit early wins.
- **Responds well to**: rank from strategic thinking, execution depth, operational reliability, innovation, leadership maturity, communication clarity, domain expertise. Base each on a signal.

## 5. Strategy

- **Tone**: more conversational for startup/scale-up builders and people who post casually; more formal for regulated industries, enterprises, very senior executives, and regions with formal norms.
- **Decision style**:
  - *Data-driven*: posts metrics, analytics/finance/quant background, talks about experiments → lead with numbers.
  - *Relationship-driven*: long tenure, mentoring, community roles, people-leadership posts → lead with collaboration, how teams worked with you.
  - *Execution-driven*: delivery titles, shipping posts, operational background → lead with what you shipped, how fast, how reliably.
- **Optimising for**: pick 1–2 of speed, stability, transformation, scaling, stakeholder management, technical depth, commercial outcomes, from the company stage and their accountability.
- **Strongest positioning**: one sentence of how the user should frame themselves for this person, plus the 2 proof points to anchor it and the 1 risk to pre-empt.
