# Coffee chat method

## 1. Professional context

- **Background**: the arc, not the list: where they started, the turns, where they are now and for how long.
- **Expertise and influence**: what they're known for (posts, talks, titles) and what they control (team, budget, hiring, vendor choices, referrals, community).
- **Priorities**: from tenure, scope and company situation (new in role → early wins and team; long tenure → depth and stability; company scaling → hiring and delivery; company cutting → efficiency).
- **Leadership-style signals**: how they write and what they share (metrics vs people stories vs shipping updates). Describe behaviour, not personality.
- **Why it matters**: what this person could realistically open up for the user (knowledge, an intro, a referral, visibility with a hiring team).

## 2. Themes

Pick 3–5: their function's current challenges, their company's recent moves, industry trends affecting their work (e.g. AI-assisted development, platform consolidation, regulation, market expansion), and the shared ground from the conversation guide. Each theme gets one line on why it will interest them.

## 3. Strategy: choose a stance

| Stance | Use when |
|---|---|
| Peer | Similar level or one step above; shared craft or domain |
| Learner | Clearly senior, or the user is moving into their field; they mentor or speak publicly |
| Collaborator | Overlapping work where you could help each other (open source, community, ecosystem) |
| Problem solver | They're visibly dealing with a challenge the user has solved before |
| Industry insider | The user has domain depth or market knowledge they'd value |

Usually lead with one and blend a second. **Realistic success after one chat**: they'd take a second conversation, share something useful (how hiring works, a name, a heads-up), or invite the user to stay in touch.

Building comfort: start with them and the shared ground, follow up on their answers instead of moving to the next question, share one relevant experience briefly, match their pace and formality, end on time.

## 4. Question quality

Good questions are about **their judgement, trade-offs, and what's next**, not facts on their profile.

- **Exploratory**: their path and decisions ("What made you move from <X> to <Y> when you did?"), what surprised them, what they'd tell someone joining their field now.
- **Business-focused**: what the company is betting on, what's hard about the current initiative, how their team's success is measured this year.
- **Industry / trends**: how a named trend is changing their work, what's overhyped, what they're watching in the next 12–18 months.
- **Career / leadership**: what separates people who grow fast on their team, how they decide whom to promote or hire, a leadership lesson from a hard period.
- **Commercial-awareness markers**: questions that connect their work to revenue, cost, customers, risk or scale.

Avoid: "What does your company do?", "What's a typical day?", "Are you hiring?", "Can you refer me?", anything their profile already answers.

## 5. Positioning

**30-second intro**: present (who you are in one line, from the value statement) → one proof point relevant to them → what you're exploring and why their perspective matters. Example shape: "I'm a <identity>. Most recently I <proof relevant to them>. I'm looking at <direction>, and your move into <their area> is close to what I'm thinking about."

**Credibility without self-promotion**: attach achievements to a story or a question ("When we cut latency on <system>, the fix wasn't where we expected; how does your team approach that?"). One per topic. Let them ask for more.

## 6. Relationship and opportunity

**Influence signals**: people-manager title, building a team, "hiring" posts, recruiters in their network engaging with their posts, recently joined as a leader, they mention open roles or headcount.

**Openness signals in the chat**: they ask about the user's work, suggest names, mention roles, say "keep me posted", run over time, share contact details.

**Follow-up ladder**:
1. Within 24 hours: thank-you referencing one specific thing they said, plus anything promised (link, article, intro) - see `follow-up` sequence E.
2. 2–4 weeks: close the loop on their advice ("I acted on what you said about <X>; here's what happened").
3. Ongoing, low-pressure: thoughtful comment on their posts, sharing a relevant article once in a while, congratulating real milestones, an update when the user hits a meaningful result.
4. When a relevant role opens or after a second conversation: a clear, small ask (referral or intro) via `referral-request`.

## 7. Risks and mistakes

- **Transactional / desperate**: bringing up the job search in the first minutes, asking for a referral unprompted, repeated mentions of urgency, sending the resume before they ask.
- **Handle carefully**: compensation, their company's layoffs or bad press, criticism of past employers (theirs or the user's), the reasons for leaving a previous job (one short, factual line only if asked).
- **Common mistakes**: no questions prepared, questions answerable from LinkedIn, interviewing them like a checklist, running over time, no next step.
- **Oversharing / overexplaining**: long career narration, justifying gaps unprompted, detailed personal circumstances, more than one story per answer.
- **Executive-presence killers**: filler and hedging ("just", "sort of", "I guess"), apologising for taking their time, rambling answers, over-agreeing, talking faster when nervous. Fix: short answers, a pause before answering, one clear point per turn, ending sentences firmly.
