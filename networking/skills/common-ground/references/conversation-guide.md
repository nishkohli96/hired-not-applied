# Conversation guide

## Six dimensions of shared ground

| Dimension | Look for | Strong example |
|---|---|---|
| Industry / domain / function | Same sector, product type, function, business environment (agency vs product, enterprise vs startup) | Both built OTT platforms |
| Career path | Similar moves: IC → lead, services → product, startup → enterprise, country moves, pivots | Both moved from client-services firms to product companies |
| Companies / ecosystems / clients | Same employer, same client, same partner ecosystem (e.g. both worked with AWS, Algolia, a bank, a broadcaster), same city tech scene | Both delivered for the same client |
| Education / certifications / affiliations | School, programme, certification, community, open-source project, meetup, alumni network | Same university or same certification body |
| Challenges | Scaling, transformation, migrations, high-traffic events, building teams, turnarounds | Both led a platform through a peak event |
| Themes / philosophies | What they post or speak about: testing culture, developer experience, AI tooling, leadership style, open source | Both write about code quality |

Rate: **strong** = same entity or same specific challenge; **medium** = same industry/function/ecosystem; **light** = adjacent theme. Two strong points beat ten light ones.

## Reading their priorities

| Signal in their profile | Likely priority now |
|---|---|
| Joined or promoted in the last 6–12 months | Proving early wins, building/reshaping the team |
| Title includes "Head of", "Director", "Manager" + team growth posts | Hiring, onboarding, delivery capacity |
| Company recently raised, launched or expanded | Scaling the product and team fast |
| Long tenure (5+ years) at one company | Institutional knowledge; influence across teams; may value loyalty and depth |
| Frequent posts on a topic | That topic is what they want to be known for |
| Speaks at events / mentors | Values giving back; responds to curiosity about their thinking |

Hiring-influence signals: people-manager title, "we're hiring" posts, building a new team or site, recently joined as a leader, mentions of headcount, recruiters in their network engaging with their posts.

## Openers, observations, questions

- **Openers**: start from the strongest shared ground or a specific piece of their work. One sentence, then hand them the floor. Example shape: "You moved from <X> to <Y> around the same time I did. What pulled you toward <Y>?"
- **Observations**: specific, informed, slightly opinionated; shows the user thinks about the same problems. Shape: "<Specific thing about their company/work> must make <hard part> interesting. When we did <similar thing>, the surprise was <insight>."
- **Exploratory questions** (pick 3): about their thinking, trade-offs and what's next. Good: "What's the hardest part of <their current initiative> that people outside don't see?", "What separates the engineers who do well on your team?", "If you were starting in <their field> today, what would you do differently?" Bad: "Are you hiring?", "Can you refer me?", "What does your company do?"
- **Credibility points**: one-liners from the proof bank, attached to a question or a story, never listed. Shape: "…which reminded me of when we cut checkout latency before a big sale; the fix wasn't where we expected."

## Topics to avoid or handle carefully

- Compensation, their company's layoffs or bad press (unless they raise it), politics, anything personal not on their professional profile.
- Criticising their company's product or a past employer of either person.
- The user's job search as the headline. Mention it once, late, framed as direction ("I'm looking at senior roles in <area>, so I'm curious how teams like yours think about…").
- Name-dropping without substance.

## Transactional-risk check

Common traps and fixes:
- Leading with the ask → lead with them; the ask, if any, comes after rapport and is small.
- Over-explaining the job search → one sentence, framed as direction.
- Rapid-fire questions → follow up on their answers; share a related experience.
- Pitching credentials → one credibility point, attached to a story, when relevant.
- Ending vague → close with a specific, low-effort next step.

## Relationship strategy

**Realistic first-conversation goals** (pick one): be remembered for a specific insight; learn how their team hires and what it values; earn permission to follow up; get a pointer to the right person; (only if they offer) a referral.

**Success after one chat** usually looks like: they'd take a second call, they share something useful (a name, a heads-up on a role, how their hiring works), or they suggest staying in touch. A referral in the first chat is a bonus, not the bar.

**Style they'll respond to** (choose with reasons from their profile):
| Style | Fits people who… |
|---|---|
| Peer-level discussion | are at a similar level or just above; build things; post about craft |
| Industry insights | post about market/industry trends; are senior or strategic |
| Problem-solving conversation | are in a new role, scaling, or posting about a current challenge |
| Mentorship positioning | are clearly senior, mentor or speak publicly, enjoy sharing their path |
| Relationship warmth | have a shared school, company or community with the user |
| Credibility / status signals | are executives or recruiters screening quickly; respond to brand names and numbers |

Close with the natural next step: a follow-up message within 24 hours referencing one thing they said (the networking plugin's `follow-up` skill, sequence E), plus when and how to stay in touch.
