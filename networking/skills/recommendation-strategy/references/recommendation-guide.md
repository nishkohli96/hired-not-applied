# Recommendation guide

## How recruiters read recommendations

- They skim for **who** wrote it (title, relationship, company) before **what** it says.
- Specific beats glowing: a named project, a number, a behaviour under pressure.
- Recency matters: recommendations from the last 2–3 years count most.
- Mix matters: all-peer sets look like favour swaps; at least one manager or client adds weight.
- For leadership targets, a former direct report's recommendation is strong evidence of how the user leads.
- Mutual "recommendation swaps" with identical wording look weak.

## Value scoring

| Factor | High | Low |
|---|---|---|
| Seniority | Manager, skip-level, client lead, executive | Junior peer far from the work |
| Credibility | Recognisable title or company, relevant field | Unrelated field, unclear role |
| Closeness | Worked together daily on the same project | Occasional contact |
| Results | Saw outcomes first-hand (launch, metric, client win) | Saw activity only |
| Relevance | Can speak to the target role's top priorities | Speaks to unrelated skills |
| Recency | Last 2–3 years | 5+ years ago |

High = strong on most factors, including relevance and results. Medium = strong on some. Low = weak relevance or closeness.

## Who validates what

| Relationship | Best positioned to speak to |
|---|---|
| Former manager | Ownership, delivery, judgement, growth, impact on team goals |
| Skip-level / executive | Business impact, strategic contribution, trust at senior level |
| Former direct report | Leadership style, mentoring, unblocking, team development |
| Peer / colleague | Collaboration, technical depth, reliability, how hard problems got solved |
| Client / stakeholder | Customer outcomes, communication, delivery under pressure, commercial impact |
| Partner / vendor | Integration work, cross-company collaboration, professionalism |

## Request message shape

1. Personal opener tied to the shared work ("Working with you on <project> at <company> is still one of the clearest examples I have of <thing>").
2. The ask, plainly: a short LinkedIn recommendation (or being a reference), and why now (target role direction, one line).
3. Easy to answer: offer a few points they could draw on, say a few lines is plenty, and that it's fine if timing doesn't work.
4. Return the favour only if it fits the relationship.

40–90 words. Channel: LinkedIn's "Ask for a recommendation" for LinkedIn recommendations (the user picks the relationship and position there), email or DM for references.

## Briefing note shape

- **Context**: what the user is targeting now and why recommendations help (one or two lines).
- **Areas you might highlight**: 2–3 themes from our work together.
- **Projects and achievements you saw**: bullets with company, period, numbers (from the resume).
- **In your own words**: explicit line that they should write whatever they genuinely think, short is fine.
No draft recommendation text.

## Portfolio assessment

Check the set (plus existing recommendations):
- Mix by relationship: manager / client / report / peer.
- Recency: anything from the last 2 years?
- Coverage of the target role's top themes (e.g. for a tech lead: delivery, technical depth, leading people).
- Diversity of companies (not all from one employer).
Ideal mix for most senior roles: 1–2 former managers, 1 client or stakeholder, 1 direct report if targeting leadership, 1 strong peer; 4–6 in total, at least half from the last 3 years.
