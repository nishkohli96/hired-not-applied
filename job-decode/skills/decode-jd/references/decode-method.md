# Decode method: ten lenses plus red flags

Work through these in order. Each later lens builds on the earlier ones. Tag every point [Explicit] (quote) or [Inferred] (state the clue).

## 1. Why the role exists

Answer: why was this headcount approved now?
- **Clues**: "new team", "first", "build from scratch" → new capability. "Backfill", "join an established team" → replacement. "Scale", "growing fast", "next phase" → growth. "Transform", "modernise", "migrate" → transformation. "Turn around", "improve", "fix" → underperformance.
- State the business need, the problems the organisation is trying to solve, and the outcomes the hiring manager will be judged on. Explain the reasoning in one line each.

## 2. Hiring manager priorities (top 5, ranked)

Rank by: position in the JD (title and first bullets weigh most), repetition, words like "must/critical/key", and how directly it serves the business need from lens 1. For each:
- **Why it matters** (link to lens 1).
- **Success looks like** (an observable outcome in 6–12 months).
- **How to prove it** (the kind of evidence: metric, artefact, story, reference).

## 3. Business challenges

Pick the 2–4 most likely from: growth, transformation, team performance, customer retention, operational inefficiency, scaling, revenue generation, technology modernisation, stakeholder alignment, risk/compliance, cost control, quality/reliability, market entry. Name the JD clue for each, and say which is most likely.

## 4. Success profile

Five dimensions: **technical capabilities**, **functional expertise**, **leadership skills**, **business skills**, **personal attributes**. Then 2–3 bullets on what would make a candidate stand out (usually: proof of having solved this exact problem at similar scale, plus one rare adjacent skill).

## 5. What keeps the hiring manager up at night

Think as the person who must deliver results after this hire.
- **Immediate (0–3 months)**: the fire this hire must put out or the deadline they must hit.
- **Medium-term (3–12 months)**: delivery, team, quality or stakeholder risks.
- **Strategic (12+ months)**: what the function must become; what the HM's own boss expects.

## 6. Critical skills and competencies

Four tiers, each ranked, each with **why it matters** and **weight** (High / Medium / Low):
- **Hard gates**: licences, degrees, clearances, work authorisation, location, language, stated minimum years. Failing one usually ends the process.
- **Must-have**: in the title, first bullets, or repeated.
- **High-value**: not required but strongly tied to the priorities; often the tie-breaker.
- **Nice-to-have**: "preferred", "bonus", "plus", "familiarity with".
Also list **implied** skills never stated but required by the duties ("own the roadmap" → stakeholder management).

## 7. ATS and keyword intelligence

Exact phrases as written in the JD, grouped:
- **Critical**: in the title, must-haves or repeated; resumes without them get filtered.
- **Important**: core duties and tools.
- **Industry**: domain nouns, regulations, standards.
- **Leadership**: lead, mentor, own, drive, hire, stakeholder.
- **Business outcome**: revenue, growth, retention, efficiency, scale, cost, quality.
Note acronym + full-form pairs ("CI/CD" / "continuous integration"), and say which 5–8 keywords matter most and why.

## 8. Interview preparation intelligence

- **Topics** most likely to be explored, tied to priorities and challenges.
- **Experiences** to have ready (situations the HM will want to hear about).
- **Achievements** most relevant (types, with the metric that would land).
- **The questions behind the questions**: what the HM is really trying to answer, e.g. Can this person solve our problem? Can they lead? Can they scale? Can they influence stakeholders? Will they stay? For each, why it's on the HM's mind.

## 9. Likely interview questions (10)

Mix by stage and domain: behavioural, functional/technical, situational, and probes on the biggest challenge. For each: **why it's likely**, **what's being evaluated**, **evidence a strong candidate brings**. No scripted answers.

## 10. Positioning (needs the resume)

Map the resume to lenses 2, 5 and 6:
- **Experiences to emphasise** and **achievements to highlight** (use the resume's proof bank; real numbers only).
- **Leadership examples** and **business-impact stories** to prepare (name the specific resume item each comes from).
- **De-emphasise**: experience that is true but off-target, or that invites a concern (e.g. an unrelated stack, over-qualification signals).
- **Gaps**: top priorities with no evidence in the resume, so the user can prepare an honest answer.

## 11. Signals and red flags

Quote the JD line for each:
- Real level vs stated title; two jobs merged into one.
- Culture signals: hours, on-call, travel, "family", "wear many hats", "fast-paced".
- Weak posting: unrealistic breadth, vague duties, salary far below market for the scope, reposted for months.
- Possible scam: fees, requests for ID/bank details before an interview, unpaid trial work, no company named, off-platform chat-only interviews.
