# Script guide

## Section patterns (adapt; never read these as templates)

**Excitement**
- Line 1: name + identity in plain words ("I'm Priya, a product designer who's spent six years on checkout flows").
- Line 2–3: the specific reason: a product move, a problem they're tackling, a line in the JD, a market they're entering. Say why it matters to you, in one sentence.
- Avoid: "I've always admired…", "I'm thrilled/passionate…", "dream job".

**Match**
- Two or three alignments, each one sentence of fact + one of meaning: "I've led a team of eight through two platform rebuilds. That's the kind of change your JD describes."
- Cover the most relevant of: industry, leadership, functional depth, business impact.
- Avoid listing tools or employers in sequence.

**Relevance**
- Name the likely problem in their terms ("scaling the product to new markets without slowing releases").
- Connect: "That's close to what I worked on at X, where …".
- Show commercial awareness: revenue, cost, customers, speed, risk.

**Achievement** (STAR, spoken)
- Challenge: one or two sentences with stakes.
- Action: what *you* did (2–3 sentences, concrete verbs).
- Outcome: the number, said naturally.
- Bridge: "That's the kind of result I'd want to bring to your team."

**Closing**
- "Thanks for watching." + one line of interest ("I'd welcome the chance to talk about how I'd approach <their problem>.") + name.
- End on a full stop, not a trailing "so… yeah".

## Writing for the ear

- 8–15 words per sentence on average; vary length.
- Contractions always (I'm, I've, that's).
- Put the important word at the end of the sentence.
- Round awkward numbers for speech when the resume allows ("94%" stays; "2,000+" becomes "over two thousand").
- No parentheses, slashes, acronyms the listener may not know, or bullet-style fragments.
- Read-aloud test: if a sentence needs a breath in the middle, split it.

## Delivery tips (pick what applies)

- **Tone**: conversational, as if explaining your work to a respected colleague.
- **Pace**: ~130 words per minute; pause briefly between sections and after the achievement's number.
- **Body language**: sit or stand upright, shoulders relaxed, hands visible occasionally; frame from mid-chest up, camera at eye level.
- **Eye contact**: look into the lens, not the screen; place notes right under the camera or use a teleprompter app.
- **Energy**: one notch above normal conversation; smile at the start and the close.
- **Setup**: quiet room, light in front of you (window or lamp), plain background, wired or lapel mic if possible, record in landscape.
- **Mistakes**: reading word for word, rushing, over-rehearsed tone, long intro, listing the resume, generic praise, going over 3 minutes, a busy background, no clear close.
- Record 3–5 takes, watch them once, pick the most natural one rather than the most polished.
