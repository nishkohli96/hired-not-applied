# How to build the answers

## Shape of a strong spoken answer

1. **Claim** (1 sentence): answer the question directly, before any story.
2. **Evidence** (2-4 sentences): one concrete situation from the resume, with what the user personally did.
3. **Outcome** (1 sentence): the number or the result. From the proof bank only.
4. **Bridge** (optional, 1 sentence): why that matters for this employer's problem.

Behavioural questions use the same shape; the evidence section carries the situation and the actions. Scenario questions replace evidence with a short approach ("first I'd…, then…"), capped at three steps, and finish with one line of precedent from the user's history.

## Length

| Question type | Spoken | Words |
|---|---|---|
| Factual (notice period, location, tools used) | 20-40 sec | 50-90 |
| Motivation and behavioural | 60-90 sec | 140-200 |
| Technical or functional depth | 60-90 sec | 140-200 |
| Scenario | 90 sec | 180-220 |

Anything longer gets interrupted. If an answer runs long, cut the setup, not the outcome.

## Tell me about yourself

Present, then the two or three moves that led here, then why this role. 60-90 seconds. Never chronological from the first job.

## Gap and risk answers

One sentence of acknowledgement, no apology. One sentence of the nearest real evidence. One sentence forward. Three sentences total.

- **Missing skill:** "I haven't used X in production. I've done Y, which solves the same problem, and here's how I'd pick X up." Only claim a learning plan the user can describe.
- **Layoff or redundancy:** state it plainly as a business decision, name the scope if the user gave it, move to what they shipped.
- **Employment gap:** say what it was, in the user's own words, and what they did during it. Never speculate on a reason the user didn't give.
- **Short tenure:** the reason the user stated, then what they delivered in the time they had.
- **Overqualified:** what specifically attracts them to this scope, in their words.

## Compensation

Never state a number the user hasn't given. Offer the sentence that defers ("I'd like to understand the full scope first; what range is budgeted?") and, if the user has a target, the sentence that anchors with their range and the basis for it.

## What disqualifies an answer

- A metric, client, title or tool that isn't in the resume.
- A claim of enjoying, believing or being passionate about something the user never said.
- Blaming a former employer, manager or colleague.
- A hedge so broad it answers nothing ("it depends on the context").
- More than 220 words.
